# Material 3 Expressive theme foundation on material3 1.5.0-alpha18

Phase F1 moves the Expressive theme onto `MaterialExpressiveTheme`, with the expressive motion scheme, the full M3E shape scale, and a type scale whose emphasized styles are real. material3 is strictly pinned to 1.5.0-alpha18 through the version catalog. The six palettes shrink to seeds, `TonalColorScheme.kt` generates complete tonal-spot schemes from them with androidx.core's HCT, and the answer colors are harmonized with whichever primary is active. The generated roles match Material Color Utilities exactly, so the problems are where existing screens meet the spec values: light on-container roles moved from about tone 10 to tone 30, and the spec's Regular headline weight thins every heading that took its weight from the old theme.

**Watch for:** **confirmed** light-mode captions drawn at 70–80 % of an on-container color drop to 3.5–4.6:1: Stats hero labels 3.5:1, answer-button intervals 4.0:1 (for every user, dynamic color included), Home summary labels 4.5:1; **confirmed** the Home greeting and the Transfer and Account headings fall from SemiBold to Regular, while the other tab titles keep an explicit SemiBold; **confirmed** four emphasized styles keep baseline letter spacing that the alpha18 tokens change; **confirmed** the light Good container is more than twice as saturated as the other ratings, and dark Again turns rust with green, teal, and amber primaries; **confirmed** the test results and screenshots on disk predate the final screen edits.

**Verdict**: NEEDS_CHANGES

## High-level view

All 48 roles that `tonalSpotColorScheme` generates match MCU's 2021 `SchemeTonalSpot` in tone, hue, and chroma for all six seeds in both modes. That includes the light on-containers at tone 30, the same four overrides `expressiveLightColorScheme()` applies in alpha18. Of the accents the old partial palettes collapsed onto primary, the screens read only `tertiary` (Learning counts, filtered deck names, the New segment of the card mix), and it now has a hue of its own. Primary is generated at chroma 36, so for blue, violet, green, and rose it is visibly more muted than the seed that the Settings swatch shows.

The opaque on-container pairs measure 7.2:1. Three components, though, draw on-container text at reduced alpha, a style tuned for the old tone-10 roles, and at tone 30 that text fails WCAG AA in light mode (**confirmed**). The worst case is the answer-button interval caption at about 4.0:1. Answer colors are generated by the app on every path, so dynamic-color users get the same result.

Harmonization is MCU's `Blend.harmonize`, but each rating keeps its source chroma. The light Good container therefore reaches chroma 49 against 13–23 for the other three. A 15° turn toward a green, teal, or amber primary also moves dark Again from red to rust (**confirmed**).

The emphasized weights match alpha18's tokens exactly, but copying the baseline styles also copies their letter spacing, which the tokens change for four styles (**confirmed**). Nothing reads an emphasized style yet. The baseline change reaches further: display, headline, and title large are now Regular, so every heading that took its weight from the old SemiBold theme thins out, while the titles that set SemiBold explicitly keep it (**confirmed**).

The explicit shape remap moves each corner by at most 2 dp. Components that read the scale themselves move further, to spec values: the 11 default-shaped OutlinedTextFields, the DropdownMenus, and the Snackbars go from 10 to 4 dp, FilterChips from 14 to 8, and dialogs from 34 to 28. No screenshot shows any of them.

The pin rationale holds against the AAR metadata. alpha18 needs compileSdk 35+, AGP 8.6+, and Compose 1.11.0-beta02+, while alpha29 already needs compileSdk 37 and AGP 9.1, and ui-test-junit4's transitive minimums sit below the catalog's androidx.test and Espresso pins. ui-test-manifest adds an exported, empty `ComponentActivity` to debug APKs only, which is the standard setup and a negligible exposure. I could not confirm which Compose version the 2026.06.00 BOM resolves to, because that BOM is not in the local Gradle cache.

The new tests pin the tone mapping, opaque-pair contrast, the harmonization math, and what the theme provides, and none of them is timing-sensitive. They check no alpha-blended pair and no real screen, and the results on disk predate the final edits (**confirmed**).

### On-container tone 30 against alpha-reduced captions

These are the three call sites. Each draws an on-container color over its own container:

```kotlin
// StatsScreen.kt, HeroTile: onPrimary/onSecondary/onTertiaryContainer over the matching container
Text(suffix, style = MaterialTheme.typography.labelSmall, color = content.copy(alpha = 0.7f))
Text(label, style = MaterialTheme.typography.labelMedium, color = content.copy(alpha = 0.7f))
// StudyComponents.kt, AnswerButton: harmonized on-container over its container
Text(option.interval, style = MaterialTheme.typography.labelMedium, color = option.onContainer.copy(alpha = 0.75f))
// HomeComponents.kt, SummaryNumber inside TodaySummaryCard: onPrimaryContainer over primaryContainer
Text(label, style = MaterialTheme.typography.labelLarge, color = base.copy(alpha = 0.8f), ...)
```

To measure them, I generated every palette's roles with MCU's HCT solver at the tones `TonalColorScheme.kt` uses, composited in sRGB as Skia does, and took the WCAG ratio. androidx's `M3HCTToColor` keeps tone within 0.2 L*, so its results differ by a few hundredths at most. In light mode the hero-tile labels measure 3.53–3.62:1 (the old palettes gave 5.48–6.18:1). The interval captions have a worst rating of 3.98–4.03:1 per palette (the old fixed answer colors gave 5.20–7.15:1), and the summary labels measure 4.49–4.57:1 (7.66–8.40:1 before). All three styles are 11–14 sp, so the 4.5:1 threshold applies, and the interval caption appears on every card studied.

Drawing these captions at full opacity gives 7.2:1. An alpha of at least 0.85 keeps the worst case at 5.0:1, while 0.8 still fails at 4.43:1. Either fix also lifts the dark hero tiles, which sit at 4.4–4.5:1 both before and after this diff. `TonalColorSchemeTest` asserts only opaque pairs, so a blended-pair assertion that uses the components' alphas is what would catch this.

### Palette seeds and harmonized answer colors

The seeds carry chroma 45–48 (teal 35.5, amber 38.4), and tonal spot generates primary at chroma 36. Primary is therefore #2F628C for the #00639B blue seed, #65558F for violet #6750A4, #35693E for green #206B34, and #8C4A60 for rose #984061. `SettingsScreen` still draws `palette.seed` as the swatch and selection ring. The KDoc calls that deliberate, but it means the swatch no longer previews the color the app will use, a separate matter from the accepted Classic-overlay drift.

`harmonizedAnswerPalettes` builds each rating's palette from its harmonized hue and its source chroma: Again 84, Hard 43, Good 49, Easy 48. At light tone 90, gamut limits cap Again and Hard near chroma 14 and Easy near 21, but green can hold its full 49. The blue theme's Good container is #8AF8C2, next to #FFD9DD, #FFDBC9, and #D0E4FF (the old hand-picked Good was #C7F0C9 at chroma 27), and the rose theme turns it lime (#BFF28E). `colors.png` shows the imbalance. In dark mode, the full 15° rotation takes Again from hue 25° to 40° for green, teal, and amber primaries. With green it becomes #7F2B00, a rust 39° from Hard's olive #604100. Capping container chroma and limiting Again's rotation would keep the four buttons balanced and keep Again red.

### Baseline weights and emphasized letter spacing

Four page headings rely on the theme weight: the greeting in `HomeComponents.kt` (headlineMedium), the heading in `TransferScreen.kt` (headlineMedium), and both headings in `AccountScreen.kt` (headlineSmall). The old theme made those SemiBold at 26 and 22 sp. They are now Regular at 28 and 24 sp. Settings, More, Stats, Browse, the Home "Decks" title, and the migration screen pass `fontWeight = FontWeight.SemiBold` explicitly. As a result, the Home tab shows a Regular greeting above a SemiBold "Decks", and the tabs disagree on header weight.

For four emphasized styles, alpha18's `TypeScaleTokens` set letter spacing that the `copy(fontWeight = ...)` versions do not pick up: bodyLargeEmphasized 0.15 sp (kept 0.5), bodyMediumEmphasized 0.25 sp (kept 0.2), titleMediumEmphasized 0.15 sp (kept 0.2), and displayLargeEmphasized 0 sp (kept −0.25). The other eleven match in size, line height, and letter spacing. Plain `Typography()` binds the 15-argument constructor, as the KDoc says. Naming any single emphasized style instead selects the Expressive constructor, which supplies the other fourteen from the tokens:

```kotlin
internal val ExpressiveTypography =
    Typography(
        // The display large emphasized token is the baseline style at medium weight and zero tracking.
        displayLargeEmphasized = Typography().displayLarge.copy(fontWeight = FontWeight.Medium, letterSpacing = 0.sp),
    )
```

`ExpressiveThemeContractTest` compares only weight and font size, so extending it to letter spacing would pin the result.
